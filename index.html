The Five Essential Microgravity Combustion Guardrails

1️⃣
Microgravity is Reduced Buoyancy, Not the Absence of Gravity
In low Earth orbit (~400 km), gravitational acceleration is ~90% of sea-level value; the microgravity state ($\approx 10^{-6}$ to $10^{-4}$ g) results from continuous free-fall. Combustion physics is altered because buoyant natural convection ($Gr \propto g$) vanishes, removing the upward plume that terraneously supplies fresh oxidizer and sweeps away combustion products.
2️⃣
Spacecraft Ventilation Creates Forced Convection Airflow
Because buoyant ventilation does not occur naturally in microgravity, crewed spacecraft (such as the ISS) continuously circulate air via fans at $5 - 20\text{ cm/s}$ to prevent CO₂ pocket accumulation. This forced airflow sustains and accelerates flame spread regimes that would otherwise self-extinguish under pure molecular diffusion.
3️⃣
Microgravity Models Cannot Automatically Predict Lunar or Martian Combustion
Partial gravity environments (Lunar $0.16g$, Martian $0.38g$) re-introduce buoyancy-driven convective flows at non-zero Grashof numbers. Mathematical models and empirical scaling rules calibrated for $10^{-5}$ g microgravity cannot be linearly scaled or assumed valid for partial gravity without re-introducing mixed-convection dimensionless parameters ($Ri = Gr / Re^2$).
4️⃣
High Measured Temperature is Not a Complete Measure of Fire Risk
Spacecraft fire safety depends on a complex interplay of limiting oxygen concentration (LOC), radiative fraction, extinction velocity, pyrolysis gas generation rate, and smoke optical obscuration. Cool flames (discovered in FLEX) sustain chemical heat release and fuel consumption below $800\text{ K}$ with zero visible emission, representing a stealth hazard invisible to standard optical flame detectors.
5️⃣
Research Gap Results Reflect the Indexed Collection Only
FLARE-X indexes a curated set of flight experiments and publication passages. An unrepresented cell in the Research Gap Matrix indicates that the condition is absent from this specific software index. It does NOT assert that NASA, international space agencies, or university researchers have not investigated that domain.

Strict Policy Against Quantitative "Fire-Risk Scores"

FLARE-X intentionally omits pseudo-quantitative "fire risk indexes" or arbitrary 1-100 severity scores. Combustion in enclosed spacecraft habitats is non-linear and context-dependent. Reducing multidimensional physics (flame spread velocity, smoke obscuration rate, toxic gas yield, and critical airflow extinction) to a single scalar score creates a dangerous illusion of safety and is scientifically unfounded.
